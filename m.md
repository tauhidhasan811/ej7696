## API 510 - Pressure Vessel Inspector: Exam Preparation Content

### I. THICKNESS MEASUREMENTS, INSPECTION INTERVALS AND VESSEL INTEGRITY

This section covers fundamental calculations and principles essential for assessing the integrity of pressure vessels, determining inspection intervals, and evaluating remaining life. These concepts are primarily derived from API 510 and ASME Section VIII, Division 1.

*   **Corrosion Rates and Inspection Intervals:**
    *   **Corrosion Rate:** The rate at which material is lost due to corrosion, typically expressed in mils per year (mpy) or millimeters per year (mm/yr). It is calculated based on current and previous thickness measurements.
    *   **Remaining Life:** The estimated time until the vessel reaches its minimum allowable thickness, calculated using the corrosion rate and the difference between the current thickness and the minimum allowable thickness.
    *   **Inspection Intervals:** The maximum time between internal, external, and on-stream inspections, determined by factors such as corrosion rates, remaining life, service conditions, and previous inspection findings, as specified in API 510.

*   **Joint Efficiencies:**
    *   **Definition:** A factor (E) applied to the allowable stress of a material to account for the efficiency of welded joints in pressure vessel construction. It reflects the integrity of the weld relative to the base metal.
    *   **Application:** Used in the design formulas for calculating minimum required thickness in ASME Section VIII, Division 1. The value of joint efficiency depends on the type of joint and the extent of nondestructive examination (NDE) performed on the weld.

*   **Static Head:**
    *   **Definition:** The pressure exerted by a column of fluid due to gravity. In pressure vessels, static head pressure must be considered, especially in tall vessels or those containing dense fluids, as it adds to the internal pressure at lower elevations.
    *   **Application:** Affects the minimum required thickness at different elevations of a vessel and is considered during pressure testing and operational assessments.

*   **Internal Pressure:**
    *   **Definition:** The pressure exerted by the fluid contained within the vessel. This is the primary design pressure for most pressure vessels.
    *   **Calculation:** Used in formulas (e.g., from ASME Section VIII, Division 1) to determine the minimum required wall thickness to withstand the hoop and longitudinal stresses induced by internal pressure.

*   **External Pressure:**
    *   **Definition:** Pressure acting on the outside surface of a vessel, which can lead to buckling or collapse, particularly in vacuum service or vessels with external jackets.
    *   **Calculation:** ASME Section VIII, Division 1 provides methods for calculating the minimum required thickness and stiffening requirements to resist external pressure.

*   **Pressure Testing:**
    *   **Purpose:** To verify the integrity and leak-tightness of a pressure vessel after construction, repair, or alteration.
    *   **Types:** Hydrostatic testing (using water) and pneumatic testing (using air or inert gas) are common.
    *   **Requirements:** API 510 and ASME Section VIII, Division 1 specify procedures, test pressures, holding times, and safety precautions for pressure testing.

*   **Impact Testing:**
    *   **Purpose:** To determine the toughness of a material at low temperatures, ensuring it can resist brittle fracture.
    *   **Requirements:** ASME Section VIII, Division 1 specifies when impact testing is required based on material type, thickness, and minimum design metal temperature (MDMT). It outlines test methods (e.g., Charpy V-notch) and acceptance criteria.

*   **Weld Size for Attachment Welds at Openings:**
    *   **Purpose:** To ensure adequate strength for nozzle and other attachment welds to the vessel shell or head.
    *   **Requirements:** ASME Section VIII, Division 1 provides rules for calculating the minimum required weld sizes (e.g., fillet weld leg dimensions) to ensure the attachment can withstand design loads and provide reinforcement.

*   **Nozzle Reinforcement:**
    *   **Purpose:** To compensate for the material removed when an opening (e.g., for a nozzle) is cut into a pressure vessel shell or head, preventing excessive stress concentration.
    *   **Requirements:** ASME Section VIII, Division 1 outlines methods for calculating the required area of reinforcement and the acceptable limits for distributing this reinforcement around the opening. This includes considering the inherent reinforcement from the nozzle neck and any added reinforcement pads.

### II. WELDING PROCEDURE AND QUALIFICATION EVALUATION

This section focuses on the requirements for qualifying welding procedures and welders, primarily based on ASME Section IX, with general requirements from ASME Section VIII, Division 1, and API 510. The scope is limited to specific welding processes and base metals.

*   **Weld Procedure Review (WPS, PQR, WPQ):**
    *   **Welding Procedure Specification (WPS):** A written document providing detailed instructions for performing a weld. It includes essential, nonessential, and supplementary essential variables (when required) as defined by ASME Section IX.
    *   **Procedure Qualification Record (PQR):** A record of the actual welding variables used during the welding of a test coupon and the results of the mechanical tests performed on that coupon. It supports the WPS and demonstrates that the procedure can produce welds with acceptable mechanical properties.
    *   **Welder Performance Qualification (WPQ):** A record demonstrating a welder's ability to produce sound welds following a qualified WPS. It documents the essential variables for the welder and the results of their qualification tests.
    *   **General Welding Requirements (ASME Section VIII, Div. 1 & API 510):** These codes provide general requirements for welding, including material compatibility, preheat and postweld heat treatment (PWHT) considerations, and acceptance criteria for weld imperfections. API 510 specifically addresses welding for repairs and alterations of in-service pressure vessels.

*   **Welding Processes Limited to:**
    *   **Shielded Metal Arc Welding (SMAW):** An arc welding process that uses a consumable electrode covered with a flux to lay the weld.
    *   **Gas Tungsten Arc Welding (GTAW):** An arc welding process that uses a non-consumable tungsten electrode to produce the weld, with an inert shielding gas.
    *   **Gas Metal Arc Welding (GMAW):** An arc welding process that uses a continuous solid wire electrode and a shielding gas.
    *   **Submerged Arc Welding (SAW):** An arc welding process where the arc is submerged under a blanket of granular flux.

*   **Base Metals Limited to P-No.:**
    *   **P-No. 1:** Carbon-manganese steels.
    *   **P-No. 3:** Half-molybdenum and half-chromium-molybdenum steels.
    *   **P-No. 4:** 1�% chromium, �% molybdenum steels.
    *   **P-No. 5:** Chromium-molybdenum steels (e.g., 2�% Cr, 1% Mo).
    *   **P-No. 8:** Austenitic stainless steels.
    *   **Purpose of P-Numbers:** ASME Section IX groups base metals into P-Numbers based on their metallurgical characteristics and weldability, simplifying the qualification process by allowing a single procedure qualification to cover a range of similar materials.

### III. NONDESTRUCTIVE EXAMINATION

This section covers the principles and application of specific Nondestructive Examination (NDE) methods as detailed in ASME Section V, which are crucial for detecting flaws and ensuring the integrity of pressure vessels.

*   **ASME Section V, Nondestructive Examination:**
    *   **Article 1 (General):**
        *   **Scope:** Provides general requirements applicable to all NDE methods, including responsibilities, personnel qualification, equipment calibration, and documentation.
        *   **Key Principles:** Emphasizes the importance of qualified personnel, proper procedures, and accurate record-keeping for reliable NDE results.
    *   **Article 2 (Radiography):**
        *   **Scope:** Details the requirements for radiographic examination, including radiation sources (X-ray and gamma ray), film or digital imaging techniques, image quality indicators (IQIs), and interpretation of results.
        *   **Application:** Used to detect internal volumetric flaws such as porosity, slag inclusions, and lack of penetration in welds and castings.
    *   **Article 6 (Liquid Penetrant):**
        *   **Scope:** Outlines the procedures for liquid penetrant examination, including surface preparation, penetrant application, dwell time, excess penetrant removal, developer application, and interpretation of indications.
        *   **Application:** Used to detect surface-breaking discontinuities in non-porous materials, such as cracks, laps, and porosity.
    *   **Article 7 (Magnetic Particle - Yoke and Prod only):**
        *   **Scope:** Specifies the requirements for magnetic particle examination, limited to the yoke and prod techniques. It covers magnetization methods, application of magnetic particles (dry or wet), and interpretation of indications.
        *   **Application:** Used to detect surface and slightly subsurface discontinuities in ferromagnetic materials, such as cracks and inclusions.
    *   **Article 23 (Ultrasonic Thickness - SE-797 only):**
        *   **Scope:** Provides requirements for ultrasonic examination for thickness measurement, specifically referencing Section SE-797. It covers equipment calibration, transducer selection, coupling, and measurement procedures.
        *   **Application:** Used to determine the wall thickness of components, crucial for corrosion monitoring and remaining life calculations.

### IV. PRACTICAL KNOWLEDGE - GENERAL & SPECIFIC

This section encompasses the practical application of various API Recommended Practices and ASME standards, providing specific guidance for inspection, damage mechanisms, repair, and material verification.

*   **API 510, Pressure Vessel Inspection Code:**
    *   **Scope:** The foundational code for in-service inspection, rating, repair, and alteration of pressure vessels. It covers inspection intervals, methods, fitness-for-service assessments, and repair procedures.

*   **API Recommended Practice 571, Damage Mechanisms Affecting Fixed Equipment in the Refining Industry (Sections: 2, 3.3, 3.8, 3.9, 3.11, 3.14, 3.15, 3.17, 3.22, 3.27, 3.35, 3.36, 3.37, 3.46, 3.61, 3.67):**
    *   **Scope:** Provides detailed descriptions of various damage mechanisms that can affect pressure vessels and piping in the refining industry. Understanding these mechanisms is critical for inspectors to identify potential degradation, predict future damage, and select appropriate inspection techniques.
    *   **Key Sections (examples):**
        *   Section 2: Introduction and general information.
        *   Section 3.3: Amine Corrosion.
        *   Section 3.8: Brittle Fracture.
        *   Section 3.9: Caustic Embrittlement.
        *   Section 3.11: Chloride Stress Corrosion Cracking (CSCC).
        *   Section 3.14: Corrosion Under Insulation (CUI).
        *   Section 3.15: Cracking, Environmental Assisted.
        *   Section 3.17: Dealloying.
        *   Section 3.22: Erosion/Erosion-Corrosion.
        *   Section 3.27: High-Temperature Hydrogen Attack (HTHA).
        *   Section 3.35: Mechanical Fatigue.
        *   Section 3.36: Metallurgical Changes.
        *   Section 3.37: Microbiologically Induced Corrosion (MIC).
        *   Section 3.46: Stress Corrosion Cracking (SCC).
        *   Section 3.61: Wet H2S Damage (Blistering/HIC/SOHIC/SSC).
        *   Section 3.67: Vibration-Induced Fatigue.

*   **API Recommended Practice 572, Inspection Practices for Pressure Vessels:**
    *   **Scope:** Provides guidance on the inspection of pressure vessels, including planning, preparation, inspection techniques, and reporting. It supplements API 510 by offering practical advice on how to conduct effective inspections.

*   **API Recommended Practice 576, Inspection of Pressure-relieving Devices:**
    *   **Scope:** Covers the inspection and repair of pressure-relieving devices (PRDs), such as pressure relief valves (PRVs). It addresses testing, maintenance, and documentation requirements to ensure PRDs function correctly and safely.

*   **API Recommended Practice 577, Welding Processes, Inspection, and Metallurgy:**
    *   **Scope:** Provides inspectors with a comprehensive understanding of welding processes, common welding flaws, inspection techniques for welds, and basic metallurgy relevant to pressure equipment. It supports the evaluation of welding procedures and welder performance.

*   **API Recommended Practice 578, Material Verification Program for New and Existing Assets:**
    *   **Scope:** Outlines requirements for establishing and implementing a material verification program (MVP) to confirm that the correct materials of construction are used and maintained in pressure equipment, preventing potential failures due to material mix-ups.

*   **ASME PCC-2, Repair of Pressure Equipment and Piping (Articles: 101, 201, 202, 209, 210, 211, 212, 215, 216, 304, 305, 312, 501, 502):**
    *   **Scope:** Provides detailed methods for the repair of pressure equipment and piping. It offers practical guidance and acceptable engineering practices for various repair scenarios.
    *   **Key Articles (examples):**
        *   Article 101: General Requirements for Repair.
        *   Article 201: Weld Overlay and Cladding.
        *   Article 202: Full Penetration Weld Repairs.
        *   Article 209: Repair of Cracks.
        *   Article 210: Repair of Dents and Gouges.
        *   Article 211: Repair of Localized Thin Areas.
        *   Article 212: Repair of Pressure Vessels and Piping by Reinforcement.
        *   Article 215: Repair of Heat Exchanger Tubes.
        *   Article 216: Repair of Pressure Vessels and Piping by Welding.
        *   Article 304: Nonmetallic Composite Repair Systems.
        *   Article 305: Mechanical Clamp Repair.
        *   Article 312: Hot Tapping.
        *   Article 501: Inspection and Examination.
        *   Article 502: Pressure Testing.
